# Profile Page UX Research — Source Notes

**Project 01 · Reference Material**
**Researched 2026-06-09 — Juan-Carlos Morales + Claude**

How peer platforms structure their people-profiles. Eleven platforms analyzed across four dimensions: **profile anatomy + module order**, **identity treatment** (how the person is centered), **trust signals**, **CTA pattern**.

This file is the *source of learnings* — what the platforms actually do. A separate `best-practices.md` (created after we agree on what to adopt) will codify the curated conclusions.

---

## Platforms reviewed

**Talent networks (5)** — Braintrust · Toptal · Catalant · Upwork · Working Not Working
**Consultancies (4)** — McKinsey · BCG · Pentagram · IDEO
**Personal landing pages (2)** — About.me · Bento.me
**Universal reference (1)** — LinkedIn

The selection deliberately covers our two competitive frontiers: the talent networks we're benchmarked against by prospects, and the consultancies we're positioned against by clients. Personal landing pages anchor the "owned-by-the-individual" archetype.

## Talent networks

### Braintrust
**Sources:** [Braintrust profile guide](https://support.usebraintrust.com/hc/en-us/articles/14302715197847-Guide-Your-Profile) · [usebraintrust.com](https://www.usebraintrust.com/)

- **Anatomy + order**: profile picture → name + location → tagline + bio → role + skills (5 minimum, top 3 as "Superpowers") → work history → past projects (up to 10, with framework: Product / Role / Contribution / Team) → certificates → experience level. Completion percentage gated — must hit 100% to apply for jobs.
- **Identity treatment**: standardized skeleton; everyone fills the same blanks. Picture + tagline are the human signal. "Superpowers" is the only personality lever in the structure.
- **Trust signals**: skills selection (with "Superpowers" prioritization), certificates, years of experience, profile completion percentage, application status (Approved Talent badge).
- **CTA pattern**: through-platform proposals only. No direct contact, no public email. The platform is the broker.
- **Distinctive moves**: "Superpowers" framing for top-3 skills (tailor them per job); explicit project framework prompting structured stories; 6-years rule for tenure signaling.

### Toptal
**Source:** [toptal.com](https://www.toptal.com/) + pattern memory (SPA, profile pages not openly indexed)

- **Anatomy + order**: photo → name → "Top 3%" badge → headline → location → availability indicator → bio → skills (verbal lists with proficiency levels) → work experience → portfolio → education. Profile is largely auto-built from the screening process.
- **Identity treatment**: "Top 3%" is the dominant identity signal — the network's brand vouches for the individual. Personality is muted; consistency wins.
- **Trust signals**: Top 3% badge (the marquee signal), Toptal-verified skills, work history with named brands, screening completion.
- **CTA pattern**: no direct contact. Clients book through Toptal sales; talent is then matched. The talent has no contact form on their page.
- **Distinctive moves**: Brand-vouches-for-individual model. Almost no self-presentation surface — Toptal does the curating.

### Catalant
**Source:** [A Model of a Great Expert Profile](https://catalant.com/managing-your-practice/a-model-of-a-great-expert-profile/)

- **Anatomy + order**: photo (encouraged, not required) → location → tagline → about → work experience → past projects (up to 10) → certifications → education → industries → project types → languages. Bias toward resume-completeness.
- **Identity treatment**: "Treat your profile like a resume." Identity is professional credentials. Tagline differentiates; about reads "like a corporate biography on a company website."
- **Trust signals**: thorough work history (incomplete profiles get declined during review), 19+ years average experience, prior major-firm logos (McKinsey, Fortune 500), Catalant verification, "Past Projects" with detailed context.
- **CTA pattern**: through-platform. Experts search for projects; clients post; matching is mutual. Profile is public-discoverable via Google but contact is platform-mediated.
- **Distinctive moves**: explicit consultant-style framing throughout ("Industries" and "Project Types" are top-level filters, not just tags). Profile completeness has teeth — failing it gets the application rejected.

### Upwork
**Source:** [Upwork enhancement guide](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016144974-How-to-enhance-your-freelancer-profile)

- **Anatomy + order**: photo + title → hourly rate → overview → skills → portfolio (highly weighted — published portfolios get 9× more hires) → work + education history → other experiences → linked social. Plus job success score, rising talent badges, earnings totals.
- **Identity treatment**: marketplace listing — title is searchable, photo is verification. Personality lives in the overview prose.
- **Trust signals**: Job Success Score (algorithmic, public), Rising Talent / Top Rated / Top Rated Plus badges, total earnings shown, client reviews + ratings, hours billed.
- **CTA pattern**: through-platform messaging only. "Invite to job" or "Hire" buttons; no email.
- **Distinctive moves**: heavy algorithmic surfacing — title + skills are search-keyword optimized. Portfolio is dominant (9× hires). Earnings + JSS are the strongest social proof on the page.

### Working Not Working
**Source:** [WNW magazine creative FAQs](https://magazine.workingnotworking.com/creative-faqs) — *Note: WNW is shutting down June 2025, merging into Fiverr*

- **Anatomy + order**: photo + bold "**Working** or **Not Working**" status header → name + role → 12-project portfolio (each: title, description, link, role, company, industry, collaborators) → skill tags → location.
- **Identity treatment**: status-led — your availability is the headline. Visual portfolio is the second pillar.
- **Trust signals**: invite-only / vetted network membership (the brand vouches), named collaborators on projects (peer proof), employer brands in portfolio (Google, Apple, Airbnb, IDEO).
- **CTA pattern**: through-platform contact (creative inquiries routed through WNW for hiring).
- **Distinctive moves**: the **"Working / Not Working" status** as the primary identity signal — pioneered the visible-availability pattern. Project-level collaborator tagging (peer co-credit).

## Consultancies

### McKinsey
**Sources:** [McKinsey people](https://www.mckinsey.com/us/our-people) + pattern memory

- **Anatomy + order**: photo → name → title (Senior Partner / Partner / etc.) → location → expertise area (e.g., "People & Organizational Performance") → narrative bio (multi-paragraph, third-person) → selected published insights → recent news/activity. Standardized template, prestige-framed.
- **Identity treatment**: institutional. The firm's brand wraps every individual. Profile reads like a biographical introduction at a board meeting. Third-person voice, formal tone, no personality flourishes.
- **Trust signals**: title (signals seniority), named client industries, published thought leadership (McKinsey Quarterly etc.), service on internal leadership councils, recognition.
- **CTA pattern**: **no contact mechanism on the profile**. The firm sells, not the individual. Clients reach McKinsey, not Sarah at McKinsey.
- **Distinctive moves**: published-insights module — the partner's *writing* substitutes for testimonials. "Our insights" as a credibility surface.

### BCG
**Source:** [BCG People & Org experts](https://www.bcg.com/capabilities/people-strategy/experts) + pattern memory

- **Anatomy + order**: photo (often black-and-white, treatment-aligned) → name → title + location → practice area chips → bio → recent publications → contact (for press only, not biz dev). Similar template to McKinsey but visually more designed.
- **Identity treatment**: same institutional framing as McKinsey, slightly warmer visual language. "We" voice dominates the firm; individual bios still third-person.
- **Trust signals**: title, practice-area chips, named industry leadership roles, published thought leadership, recognition.
- **CTA pattern**: same as McKinsey — no individual contact. Press contact only.
- **Distinctive moves**: practice-area chips (more readable than McKinsey's text-only categorization). Visually styled portraits (vs. uniform headshots).

### Pentagram
**Source:** [Pentagram — Michael Bierut](https://www.pentagram.com/about/michael-bierut)

- **Anatomy + order**: portrait (large, square, treated) → "**[Name]  [Location]**" header → multi-paragraph narrative bio (career arc, notable work, awards, teaching, writing) → recent news (talks, podcasts, press appearances) → publications → videos.
- **Identity treatment**: the most prestige-framed of all 11 platforms. The bio is a *biography*, not a profile. Reads like a museum-catalog entry. Pentagram brands itself as a "creative collective" of 23 partners — the individual is centered, but inside a tight institutional frame.
- **Trust signals**: named iconic clients (Mastercard, Slack, Hillary Clinton campaign), prestigious awards (AIGA Medal, Hall of Fame), institutional roles (Yale critic, founded Design Observer), published books, video appearances at conferences.
- **CTA pattern**: **no individual contact**. New business inquiries route to the office email (`newyork@pentagram.com`). The partner does not have a public sales surface.
- **Distinctive moves**: the **"recent news" stream** — Bierut's page shows interviews, talks, podcast appearances. The partner is continually "in conversation" — recency = relevance. Citizenship-of-the-field is itself a trust signal.

### IDEO
**Sources:** [IDEO leaders](https://www.ideo.com/leaders) · [HubSpot best meet-the-team pages](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/creative-agency-team-pages) + pattern memory

- **Anatomy + order**: photo (often candid, lifestyle-aligned) → name → title or hyphenated identity ("designer, doer, storyteller, listener, problem solver") → personal narrative bio with non-work color (where they live, what they care about) → approach / craft framing → selected work or themes.
- **Identity treatment**: most personality-forward of the consultancies. IDEO leans into the "people first" frame — bios mention lived experience, communities, what brought them to design. Title is often hyphenated identity, not job hierarchy.
- **Trust signals**: design background depth, named approaches (e.g., "data visualization, XR, space design"), institutional teaching/practice combo, the IDEO brand itself.
- **CTA pattern**: no individual contact. Inquiries route to firm.
- **Distinctive moves**: the **lifestyle/personality-forward bio** — non-work content treated as relevant, not optional. "Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion shaped their perspective through nearly a decade living in intentional communities." This is closer to *humanizing the consultant* than any other firm.

## Personal landing pages

### About.me
**Source:** [about.me](https://about.me/)

- **Anatomy + order**: photo → name → headline (e.g., "Construction Safety Professional in Nashville, Tn") → bio (3–5 paragraphs) → link grid → contact form.
- **Identity treatment**: maximum personal control; minimal templating. Each person chooses what to surface.
- **Trust signals**: certifications mentioned in bio (OSHA 500, etc.), credentials embedded, linked employer / project URLs.
- **CTA pattern**: contact form on page → goes direct to the owner. Also "Hire me" buttons linking to LinkedIn.
- **Distinctive moves**: new "**AI Twin**" feature — a chatbot trained on the person that visitors can ask questions to. Most-active conversion mechanism we've seen. "Available 24/7 / Captures leads automatically / Never misses a lead."

### Bento.me
**Sources:** [bento.me](https://bento.me/en/home) · [landing](https://landing.bento.me/) — *Note: shutting down February 2026*

- **Anatomy + order**: photo + headline → modular grid of cards (links, embedded media, social posts, products to sell). Drag-and-drop layout.
- **Identity treatment**: maximum visual customization within a constrained grid system. The grid IS the brand — recognizable Bento aesthetic.
- **Trust signals**: linked social proof (Instagram follower counts, etc.), embedded best work, integrated product/shop.
- **CTA pattern**: link-in-bio aggregator. CTAs are whatever the user puts on their cards — usually "Buy" or "Subscribe" or external links.
- **Distinctive moves**: **rich grid of card modules** instead of linear scroll. Each card is a different content type (link, image, video, product, embedded post).

## Universal reference: LinkedIn

**Sources:** [LinkedIn anatomy guides](https://medium.com/@katarinanahtigal/the-anatomy-of-a-high-impact-linkedin-profile-a63d73bca861) + pattern memory

- **Anatomy + order**: cover image → photo → headline (industry-search-weighted) → contact info → about → **Featured section** (pinned posts, articles, links, media) → experience → education → licenses & certifications → skills (endorsed by network) → recommendations → activity → publications/projects.
- **Identity treatment**: hybrid — title signals to algorithm, headline sells to humans, featured section showcases voice. About + Featured is where personality shows; everything else is structured.
- **Trust signals**: connections count, endorsement counts on skills, written recommendations from named colleagues, recent activity, follower count for active posters.
- **CTA pattern**: connect / message / follow / "Open to work" badge. Direct messaging available (subject to relationship). No external email shown publicly (by default).
- **Distinctive moves**: **Recommendations** — written, named, mutually visible. Strongest written-testimonial pattern in the set. **Featured section** is the most-underused-but-highest-impact module on the site.

## Cross-cutting patterns

### Universals (appear in ≥4 platforms across all categories)
1. **Photo + name + headline** — every platform leads with this triplet
2. **Bio / overview** — narrative paragraph(s), tone varies by platform
3. **Work history or "selected work"** — listed projects with context (Catalant explicit framework; Pentagram named clients)
4. **Location** — surprisingly universal; matters for matching even for remote work
5. **Skills, tags, or expertise areas** — some structured form of "what can this person be hired for"
6. **Recognition or credentials** — varies wildly in form but every platform has *something*

### Talent-network signatures (recur across Braintrust, Toptal, Catalant, Upwork, WNW)
- Standardized profile skeleton — everyone fills the same blanks
- Heavy keyword/skill tagging for algorithmic matching
- Public completion percentage, ratings, badges
- Through-platform contact only — no direct email
- The talent self-manages; the platform mediates
- Identity is "service provider"; the question on the page is *what can you do for me?*

### Consultancy signatures (recur across McKinsey, BCG, Pentagram, IDEO)
- Narrative bios (long-form, third-person, biographical)
- Named clients function as testimonials (no quotes, just logos / mentions)
- Published thought leadership as trust surface (insights, books, talks)
- Recognition is institutional (awards, hall of fame, named roles)
- **No individual contact mechanism** — the firm sells
- The firm manages; the individual is presented
- Identity is "expert"; the question on the page is *who are you?*

### The consultant-vs-talent-network split (the real fork in the road for Chameleon)

| Dimension | Talent network | Consultancy |
|---|---|---|
| **Profile structure** | Standardized skeleton, fill the blanks | Narrative, rich per-person variation |
| **Identity framing** | Service provider — *what can you do?* | Expert — *who are you?* |
| **Trust signals** | Algorithmic — ratings, badges, completion % | Institutional — awards, named clients, publications |
| **Work display** | Portfolio gallery + project frameworks | Named clients, selected work, narrative |
| **Skills / tagging** | Heavy — search-optimized | Light — narrative-driven |
| **CTA path** | Through-platform messaging | None — firm sells, not the individual |
| **Self-managed** | Yes — by the talent | No — managed by the firm |
| **Discovery flow** | Search / filter / match | Browse partners; no real search |
| **Risk** | Becoming a commodity in a listing | Becoming an inaccessible institution |

### What sits between them (where Chameleon actually wants to be)
- **Consultancy-style identity** — the page should feel like a real person, not a listing
- **Talent-network discoverability** — search has to work; clients shouldn't need to know the name to find the Chameleon
- **A real CTA path** — neither consultancies' "no contact" nor talent networks' "through-platform only." Both fail us. We need variable opt-in routing (per [Idea 14](ideas.html#idea-14))
- **Curated tagging, not exhaustive** — light enough to read as narrative, heavy enough to power search (per [Idea 23](ideas.html#idea-23))
- **Trust signals borrowed from both columns** — testimonials (the LinkedIn / WNW pattern), named clients (the Pentagram pattern), no public star ratings (rejected per [Idea 22](ideas.html#idea-22))

## Pre-filter through the Chameleon Rubric

A first pass on which patterns clearly *pass* or *fail* the rubric for Chameleon. This is research-side filtering; the curated best-practices will go deeper.

### Strong fit (rubric-aligned)
- **Narrative bio** (consultancies) — humanizes; respects the person; aligns with "centers people, not pages."
- **Featured / pinned content section** (LinkedIn) — Chameleon-curated proof; gives autonomy.
- **Named clients as logos** (Pentagram + WNW) — credible without listing every job.
- **Published thought leadership / recent activity stream** (Pentagram, McKinsey) — passive promotion, requires no Chameleon hustle, compounds over time.
- **Project framework prompting** (Braintrust's Product/Role/Contribution/Team) — structures Chameleons' stories without writing them for them.
- **Status / availability indicator** (WNW) — solves the real pain (Sonia is at 50% looking for work). Pairs with [Idea 17](ideas.html#idea-17).
- **Recommendations / written testimonials** (LinkedIn) — already-validated trust signal; aligns with [Idea 4](ideas.html#idea-04).
- **Modular page architecture** (Bento) — opt-in customization; aligns with [Idea 1](ideas.html#idea-01).

### Weak or risky fit (rubric flags)
- **Algorithmic star ratings** (Upwork's JSS) — already flagged C+ on the Ideas page; cuts against "We over Me."
- **Brand-vouches-for-individual model** (Toptal's "Top 3%") — over-centralizes; reduces individual differentiation; the badge becomes the person.
- **Standardized skeleton with completion gating** (Braintrust, Catalant) — feels bureaucratic; the page becomes a form, not a story.
- **No CTA on profile** (consultancies) — fails the core thesis: the page exists to help prospects decide whether to reach out.
- **Heavy keyword stuffing for SEO** (Upwork) — already what we have today; the brief explicitly rejects this.
- **AI Twin chatbot** (About.me) — high novelty, low signal that prospects want this; risky as a primary CTA.

### Worth testing (interesting, rubric-neutral)
- **Tailored "Superpowers"** (Braintrust) — top-3 skills changeable per pitch. Lightweight, gives autonomy.
- **Recent news / activity stream** (Pentagram) — could be powerful or could be empty (depends on whether Chameleons are actively creating content).
- **"Hyphenated identity" headlines** (IDEO) — humanizes but feels twee at scale; for some Chameleons not others.
- **Named collaborators on projects** (WNW) — peer co-credit is interesting but related to the rejected "common collaborators" tag. Needs the same scrutiny.

## What I'm bringing back for discussion

Three big questions for us to align on before I write the curated `best-practices.md`:

1. **How far do we lean consultancy vs. talent network?** My read: Chameleon's profile should *feel* like a Pentagram / IDEO partner page (narrative, prestige-framed, humanized) but *work* like a talent network (searchable, contactable, status-aware). That's the contested ground — every adopted pattern should be tested against it.

2. **Which trust signals do we adopt, which do we explicitly reject?** Testimonials yes (already aligned). Named-client logos yes (already in [Idea 5](ideas.html#idea-05)). Star ratings no (already in [Idea 22](ideas.html#idea-22)). Activity streams / thought leadership — this is the open question for me. Powerful when the Chameleon writes, embarrassing when they don't.

3. **How prescriptive should the page template be?** Braintrust forces 100% completion before applying. Pentagram has no template — it's a narrative. We've already aligned on modular + strong defaults; but how strongly do we *encourage* completion vs. let pages be sparse?
