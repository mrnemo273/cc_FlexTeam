Profile UX research — peer platforms

To inform our design, we benchmarked eleven peer platforms across four dimensions. The point isn't to copy any one of them — it's to triangulate. What we hear from Chameleons + what we see on the street + what we believe clients are looking for = what we should build.

The triangulation lens

Three signals meet here. Every committed decision should be defensible against all three.

Chameleon voices

8 Chameleons across 3 sessions told us the page is invisible, tag walls are noise, defaults must work, every Chameleon already customizes per pitch manually, testimonials are the missing piece. See the Voices page.

Market patterns

11 platforms split into two camps with almost no middle. Talent networks: searchable, status-aware, contactable. Consultancies: premium, narrative, prestige-framed. Chameleon's position sits between — and the framing principle falls out of this.

What clients want

Jeff (S3) named it: proven results, succinctly-communicated experience, personalization. Pamela: style fit, culture, industry match. Chris: “are you the right problem solver?” Triangulated below.

OutputThe Feel consultancy. Work talent network. framing principle is the synthesis. Every adopted pattern, every rejected pattern, every scored idea ties back to one or more of these three sources.

The 11 platforms, at a glance

Each platform was analyzed across four dimensions: profile anatomy + module order, identity treatment, trust signals, CTA pattern. One-line takeaway per platform; full per-platform breakdown in research-notes.md.

Talent networks · 5
Braintrust
Standardized skeleton + “Superpowers” framing for top-3 skills, Product/Role/Contribution/Team project template. Tagline + Superpowers are the only personality levers. No direct contact — platform brokers everything.
Toptal
“Top 3%” badge dominates the identity — the brand vouches for the individual. Almost no self-presentation surface. No contact on the profile; Toptal sales matches you.
Catalant
“Treat your profile like a resume.” Heavy on work history; Industries and Project Types as top-level filters. Incomplete profiles get rejected during review. Through-platform contact only.
Upwork
Job Success Score + Top Rated badges + total earnings shown publicly. Portfolio is dominant (published portfolios get 9× more hires). Marketplace listing aesthetic. Through-platform messaging only.
Working Not Working — shutting down 2025
Pioneered the visible “Working / Not Working” availability status as the primary identity signal. Project-level collaborator tagging. Invite-only signaling.
Consultancies · 4
McKinsey
Institutional, prestige-framed, third-person narrative bio. No contact on profile — the firm sells, not the partner. Published insights substitute for testimonials. The firm's brand wraps every individual.
BCG
Same institutional pattern as McKinsey but visually warmer. Practice-area chips (more readable than text categorization). Press contact only — no biz-dev path on profile.
Pentagram
The most prestige-framed of all 11 platforms. The bio is a biography, not a profile. “Recent news” stream — talks, podcasts, press. Citizenship of the field as trust signal. New business routes to office email.
IDEO
Most personality-forward consultancy. Hyphenated identity titles (“designer, doer, storyteller”). Lifestyle/personal bio content treated as relevant, not optional. No individual contact.
Personal landing pages · 2
About.me
Direct contact form on every page (a rarity). New “AI Twin” chatbot — visitors ask questions, the trained-on-the-person bot answers 24/7. Most-active conversion mechanism we found.
Bento.me — shutting down Feb 2026
Modular card grid instead of linear scroll. Each card a different content type (link, image, video, product, embedded post). Drag-and-drop layout. The grid IS the brand.
Universal reference · 1
LinkedIn
Strongest written-testimonial pattern in the set — Recommendations are named, mutually-visible, and rare enough to carry weight when present. Featured section is the most-underused-but-highest-impact module. Title signals to algorithm, headline sells to humans.

The four dimensions we analyzed

Every platform was scored on these four cuts. Different platforms optimize for different ones; the patterns that recurred told us where the real choices live.

Profile anatomy + module order
What modules are on the page, in what sequence, and which are mandatory vs optional. Surfaces what each platform chose to emphasize. Talent networks tend toward standardized skeletons (everyone fills the same blanks); consultancies tend toward narrative variation. The Chameleon answer: modular widget builder with strong defaults — the skeleton is shared, but each Chameleon can layer personal upgrades.
Identity treatment
How the platform centers the individual person. Toptal subsumes them under the brand (“Top 3%”). Pentagram lets them shine through biography. IDEO leads with personality. Upwork treats them as a marketplace listing. The choice signals what the platform thinks the visitor cares about — the firm, the platform, or the person.
Trust signals
What carries credibility on the page. Algorithmic ratings (Upwork JSS), institutional badges (Toptal Top 3%), named clients (Pentagram), peer recommendations (LinkedIn), published thought leadership (McKinsey, Pentagram), automated client testimonials (the gap — no one does this well). Every platform leans on one or two; rarely all.
CTA pattern
How visitors actually contact the person. This is where the talent-network / consultancy split is starkest. Talent networks: through-platform messaging only (no email visible). Consultancies: no individual contact at all (firm sells). Personal landing pages: direct form. Chameleon needs none of these defaults wholesale — the four-tier opt-in routing system (Idea 14) is the answer that respects every Chameleon's preference.

The real fork in the road — consultant vs talent network

The platforms split into two camps with almost no middle ground. This table is the most-useful artifact from the research because it makes the choice explicit. Chameleon's position is “neither freelance nor agency” — which is exactly between these columns.

What sits between them — where Chameleon actually wants to be:

  • Consultancy-style identity — the page feels like a real person, not a listing
  • Talent-network discoverability — search has to work; clients shouldn't need to know the name to find the Chameleon
  • A real CTA path — neither consultancies' “no contact” nor talent networks' “through-platform only.” Both fail us. Variable opt-in routing per Chameleon's preference (Idea 14)
  • Curated tagging, not exhaustive — light enough to read as narrative, heavy enough to power search (Idea 23)
  • Trust signals borrowed from both columns — testimonials (LinkedIn / WNW pattern), named clients (Pentagram), no public star ratings (rejected — Idea 22)

What we hear clients are looking for

Triangulating with the Chameleons themselves: when Juan asked “what do you think your clients are looking for in your materials to decide?”, the answers cluster cleanly across roles. This is the third leg of the triangulation.

1 · Proven results

Concrete evidence of work that produced outcomes. Named clients, work samples, case studies, testimonials.

“Proven results” — Jeff Blettner, S3. Pamela also: “style of design matches what they're looking for.”

2 · Right-fit problem solver

Industry relevance, stage relevance, the sense that this person has solved this specific problem before.

“Are you the right problem solver for the problem I have?” — Chris Baccus, S3.

3 · Personalization

The sense that the materials were tailored to them — addressing their actual brief, not a generic capability set.

“Experience communicated succinctly and effectively + personalization” — Jeff Blettner. Drove Ideas 25 + 26.

4 · Culture fit

A read on how this person works, communicates, and shows up — first in the materials, then sealed in conversation.

“Culture fit is a huge thing” — Pamela Alegria, S3. First in materials, then conversation.

5 · Credibility wrapper

A sense that this person is backed by something — that they're vouched-for, not freelance-alone.

“I don't have that backup credibility of Chameleon Collective when I send my pamelagria.com” — Pamela Alegria, S3.

Patterns we adopted, patterns we rejected

Adopted
  • Narrative bio (consultancies) — humanizes; aligns with “centers people, not pages”
  • Featured / pinned content section (LinkedIn) — Chameleon-curated proof; gives autonomy
  • Named clients as logos (Pentagram + WNW) — credible without listing every job
  • Recent activity stream (Pentagram, McKinsey) — passive promotion, requires no Chameleon hustle
  • Project framework prompting (Braintrust's P/R/C/T) — structures Chameleons' stories without writing them
  • Status / availability indicator (WNW) — solves Sonia's pain directly
  • Written testimonials (LinkedIn) — already-validated trust signal
  • Modular page architecture (Bento) — opt-in customization
  • Tailored “Superpowers” (Braintrust) — top-3 changeable per pitch
Rejected (with rationale)
  • Algorithmic star ratings (Upwork JSS) — cuts against “We over Me”; refined out as Idea 22
  • Brand-vouches-for-individual model (Toptal Top 3%) — over-centralizes; reduces differentiation; the badge becomes the person
  • Standardized skeleton with completion gating (Braintrust, Catalant) — the page becomes a form, not a story
  • No CTA on profile (consultancies) — fails the core thesis
  • Through-platform-only messaging (talent networks) — fails the “name the contact” mandate
  • Heavy keyword stuffing for SEO (Upwork) — exactly what the brief rejects
  • AI Twin chatbot (About.me) — high novelty, no signal prospects want this as primary CTA
  • Common collaborators tag — surfaced as Idea 12, killed by Chameleons as “clicky”
