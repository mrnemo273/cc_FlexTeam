The nine questions

In conversational order. Open with pain → move to aspiration → surface existing creative output → test the landing-moment hypothesis → end on the harder brand-tension question once they're warmed up.

  1. Pain — How do you currently pitch yourself to a prospect? Where does it get clunky?
  2. 60-second test — If a prospect spends 60 seconds on your page, what three things must they take away?
  3. Landing moment — Has anyone ever told you they found you through your Chameleon page? Tell me about that moment.
  4. Cold prospect — If a prospect lands on your page cold, what's the bar they need to clear before reaching out?
  5. Reference — Show me a profile — anyone's, anywhere — that you wish yours looked like. Why?
  6. Maintenance reality — When did you last update a professional bio anywhere? Walk me through that moment.
  7. Risk check — Stepping back, is this even the right thing to invest in? What's the risk of doing it? (Round 2 only.)
  8. MVP — If we shipped Phase 1 tomorrow, what's the minimum it needs to do to be useful?
  9. Dream — No technical limits — what's the one thing this page could do for you?

Methodology + working notes

The interview design separates need discovery from concept validation. This protected Round 1 from anchoring on Austin's concept — the goal was to surface what Chameleons actually want a profile to do, not whether they like the version that already exists. Need discovery worked — sessions produced concept-shaped ideas organically from Chameleon voices, without ever showing them the reference.

Methodology Adaptation
All four Round 1 sessions ran as groups (4, 5, 5, 4 attendees) rather than 1:1s as originally planned — Chameleons had limited time and 1:1 wasn't viable. We adapted: smaller groups when possible, async pre-read of priming questions, async follow-up after each session inviting people to add what they didn't get to say in the room, explicit dissent solicitation. We accept the tradeoff and flag it honestly in synthesis rather than pretend we've fully heard every voice. Round 2 will use the same adapted approach — Chameleon time remains the constraint.
Round 1 retrospective — sample we got
Final sample landed: 4 creatives, 3 strategy/operations, 3 hybrid growth-marketers. Session 4 closed a known gap by bringing in two senior growth practitioners (Farzana, Chris Nella) who confirmed the patterns we'd been seeing. Still less represented: dedicated delivery-only practitioners and Expert Network members. Round 2 (concept reaction) should explicitly invite one of each to test whether the concept reads differently to those who don't self-promote. Findings will be folded into the synthesis without overwriting prior conclusions.
